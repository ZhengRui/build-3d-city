# London landmark facades: photo research

Companion to `landmark_facades.csv` (127 rows, the columns the engine reads: `name, facade, tint, module, variant, profile, whole, take, roof, notes`), to `landmarks_london.csv` (the 127 heights and points) and to the reference photos in `demos/data/london/refs/` (git-ignored; `refs/index.md` lists every file, its Commons page, licence, author, date and what it shows: aerial, street, day, dusk, night). Written for the London M4 (facade styles and landmark massing), 2026-10-01, modelled on Paris's `landmark_facades.md`. Method: Wikimedia Commons (API search and categories, contact sheets viewed by eye, 960 px copies), Wikipedia EN (fetched 2026-10-01) and web search for dimensions, the OSM `building:part`s near each landmark (Overpass, 2026-10-01) and Overture heights (raw/overture parquet) for what the engine will actually get, and pixel sampling of the photos (script `samp2.py`: box as image fractions `x0,y0,x1,y1`, origin top left; sky pixels dropped where b > r+25 and b > g+8 (only when 'nosky'); mean, median, `mid40-90` = median of the 40-90 % lightness band, `bright30` = mean of the brightest 30 %). Every value marked (sampled) was measured on the named file and box; (est.) is a judgement; nothing is invented as sampled.

**Coverage.** All 127 rows of `landmarks_london.csv` have a row (the must-haves, every tower of 100 m or more, the big monuments, the structures). 30 have a detailed note and a photo-sampled colour (section 1); the other rows are family rows (Canary Wharf, Nine Elms, City, Southbank, post-war towers) whose tints are estimates unless the note names a sampled file. The rows whose building does not exist (1 Undershaft, not built; St Helen's, being demolished; Aspen, One Thames City, River Park, Keybridge, No. 9 Thames City, Sky Gardens, 2 Finsbury Avenue East, One Crown Place South: no footprint matched) and the structures (London Eye, O2 masts, Cutty Sark, HMS Belfast, cable car) document the look for `[[structures]]`: `06_tiles` will print 'no building named ...' for them, as Paris did for the Eiffel Tower. Styles used: `bands` 49, `glass` 34, `monument` 13, `floodlit` 8, `concretepanel` 7, `lattice` 4, `panel` 3, `factory` 3, `fins` 2, `brickstone` 2, `stone` 1, `limestone` 1.

## How the rows are read by the engine

- `name` = `name_zh` in `landmarks_london.csv`; it applies to every building row with that name (checked by reading the list; the engine's own `landmark_facades()` was not run on a built London table: there is none yet).
- `facade` holds only styles that exist in `europe-west.toml [facade.styles]` today (glass, fins, bands, piers, lattice, panel, stone, monument, limestone, concretepanel, floodlit, brickstone, factory, ...). **NEW STYLE names are not used in the `facade` column**: `tiles.py landmark_facades()` tolerates an unknown style (it prints 'no style x in [facade.styles] yet: the buildings keep theirs, with the tint') but then the landmark keeps its own default style, which is worse than a close existing one. The proposals appear in the notes as ``NEW STYLE `x` proposed (fallback y)``; the builder adds the style to `[facade.styles]` and `facade.js` (same order) and then flips the cell. Table of proposed styles below.
- `tint` on the glass-wall styles (`glass`, `fins`, `bands`, `piers`, `lattice`) is the glass colour; on `stone`, `limestone`, `monument`, `concretepanel`, `factory`, `brickstone`, `floodlit` it is the wall colour. Sampled glass photographs carry sky: the tints are between the overcast and the blue-sky samples, as the skill's 'tint caveats' say.
- `profile` is ignored for a landmark made of OSM parts unless `whole=true`; the directives used: `crown@m[-top]:<profile>[:#hex]` (Elizabeth Tower, St Paul's, Canary Wharf Tower; all untested with London's parts), `long:` (Walkie-Talkie), plain `f:scale` profiles (Shard, Gherkin, Vase). `roof` = colour of crowns and spires. `take` (metres) is set on the landmarks whose towers, domes and turrets are unnamed OSM parts (St Paul's 6, Elizabeth Tower 4, Palace 4, Abbey 5, Tower Bridge 6, Tower of London 3, Tate 3-4, Battersea 8, Buckingham 3, Monument 3, ORNC 4, National Gallery 3): the parts inside or within that distance of the named outline join the landmark and take its row; each needs a look for neighbours that join by mistake (Battersea's 8 m is the widest). `whole=true` is set on the Shard and the Gherkin only.
- London's buildings table is not built yet: whether `min_h` is kept on parts (`lift_parts = true`) decides St Paul's, Tower Bridge, the Elizabeth Tower and the Monument: each is a stack of OSM parts with `min_height` (see the notes). Paris found `min_h = 0` on every part after M2 and needed lift_parts; London needs the same check on the first built table.

## 5. What the engine can and cannot do for these (gaps found)

1. **A profile scales the actual footprint about its centroid** with the same factor at every height: the Shard's asymmetric shards, the Cheesegrater's one-sided 10-degree lean (a vertical service tower on the east side), the Walkie-Talkie's shifted columns, the Scalpel's cut and the Vase's tilt can only be approximated (`long:` for a symmetric trapezoid). `whole=true` is needed for part-built landmarks (Shard, Gherkin); it merges only the rows that carry the landmark's name (tiles.py), so the parts must be named first (take, or the landmark name applied to the parts).
2. **OSM `roof:shape` (skillion, pyramidal, dome, gabled) is used by the London landmarks' parts (Cheesegrater wedge, Scalpel, Shard's skillions, Tower Bridge caps, the Elizabeth Tower spire, Palace roofs): whether `04_buildings`/`06_tiles` honours OSM `roof:shape` and `roof:height` (Paris took roof shapes from APUR only) must be checked**; if not, all of these are flat tops and crowns need `crown@` rows or a generic OSM roof-shape reader.
3. **`min_h`** (see above): St Paul's dome stack, Tower Bridge towers, the Monument and Big Ben are stacks of parts with `min_height`.
4. **Open lattice and curved structures:** London Eye, the O2 masts and cables, Tower Bridge's bascules (no kind), the cable car, Hungerford and Millennium bridges (cables in `suspension` only for two towers), the Shard's open spire top, the Gherkin's diagrid (lattice fallback), Heron/Broadgate/Cheesegrater exoskeletons.
5. **No Portland stone or stock brick style** (proposals above); **`crown@` is grey by default**: the St Paul's lead dome, gilded Big Ben ribs, copper Tower of London caps need the `:#hex` argument.
6. **Stone relief**: Big Ben's and Parliament's Gothic tracery, the Abbey's rose window, Buckingham's pilasters are a `monument` bay grid only.
7. **Night:** floodlit palettes (amber Palace `#a28962`, cream Abbey `#ab9a7d`, Buckingham `#948c80`, National Gallery gold `#bf9f4d`, Tower 42 LED, Gherkin warm lit `#939578`, Canary Wharf `#959f96`, London Eye red, Tower Bridge warm-white with blue chains): these are rough lit-pixel medians of the night photos (not calibrated); `night.js` needs London's own palette, and the Shard's spire and Canary Wharf's aircraft beacons as lights.
