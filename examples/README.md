# Example cities

The configuration of seven finished cities built with this skill. Configuration only: no downloaded data, no tiles,
no models, no `web/` links to the engine. Use them as starting points and as worked answers to "how did a city of
this kind set X?". Every key is documented, with its default, in `../engine/pipeline/README.md` (pipeline) and
`../engine/viewer/README.md` (viewer, `city.json`).

| City | Area | Buildings from | Region preset | Notable techniques |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `nyc` | Manhattan (87 km²) plus DUMBO-Fort Greene and the Hudson's New Jersey shore (Hoboken, Weehawken, Union City), about 110 km² | NYC official footprints with roof heights (ArcGIS layer), Overture (USGS lidar heights) in New Jersey, OSM building parts for landmarks | `us-northeast` | suspension bridges (`kind = "suspension"`), the Statue of Liberty as a `figure`, landmark facades from a CSV, a long research notes file on trees, bridges and waterfronts |
| `paris` | the 20 arrondissements (105 km²) with La Défense and the Axe historique through Neuilly | APUR building parts with measured heights, IGN BD TOPO | `europe-west` | the Seine and canals split into pools that step down at dams and locks, the Eiffel Tower as a `lattice_tower`, columns as `figure`s, gravel parks, stone parvis |
| `london` | the City, Westminster, the South Bank, Tower Hamlets west, the Isle of Dogs, Battersea, south Camden, Greenwich (64 km²) | OSM footprints with Carbon & Place heights (lidar DSM − DTM) | `uk-london` | city-only scripts `scripts/m<N>_*.py`, structures written by a script into `generated/` and `include`d (the London one is over 200 KB and left out here: run `scripts/m4h_structures.py`), street lamps by road class |
| `berlin` | Mitte, Tiergarten, Moabit, Hansaviertel, Friedrichshain, Kreuzberg, Alt-Treptow, east Charlottenburg (53 km²) | Berlin LoD2 CityGML with Umweltatlas heights, DGM1 1 m terrain | `de-berlin` | CityGML LoD2 roofs, several `generated/` structure files (the biggest, `structures-m4h.toml`, left out: run `scripts/m4h_structures.py`), buses only on bus routes |
| `singapore` | Marina Bay, the CBD, the Civic District, Orchard, Kallang Basin, HarbourFront, Mount Faber, Sentosa (33 km²) | OSM + HDB footprints and storeys + URA Master Plan footprints, estimated heights for OSM's untagged buildings | `sg-singapore` | a band of low masses round the edge plus tall masses beyond it (`masses_within`, `masses_tall_within`), raised decks (MBS SkyPark), a procedural Merlion mesh (`scripts/m7_merlion.py`) |
| `hongkong` | Victoria Harbour's both shores to the Lion Rock ridge, Tseung Kwan O, Clear Water Bay, Sai Kung (about 135 km²) | Lands Department building footprints and heights | `hk-hongkong` | steep terrain with reclaimed land, terrain pits re-filled from outlines, a painted roofscape beyond the band instead of masses, beaches reaching into the sea |
| `tokyo` | the core (Marunouchi to Shiodome, Roppongi, Tsukishima, Daiba) with Asakusa-Skytree and Shinjuku-Shibuya islands (49 km²) | PLATEAU LOD1 CityGML (one GML per 1 km mesh and ward), OSM fill | `jp-tokyo` | a ring of town masses beyond the districts (`masses_ring` with a thinning edge), tall masses over the plain, GSI 5 m DEM, projecting vertical signs generated by a script (`structures-m4f-signs.toml`, 410 KB, left out: run `scripts/m4f_signs.py`) |

Region presets live in `../engine/pipeline/src/city3d/presets/<name>.toml` (pipeline tables: building kinds,
species, cars, rooftops, road widths) and `../engine/viewer/styles/<name>.json` (facade styles, viewer defaults).

## What each folder holds

```
<city>/
  city.toml          every city-specific pipeline setting; the comments say why each value is what it is
  pyproject.toml     a uv project depending on the engine by relative path (see below)
  web/city.json      the viewer's settings for this city: title, About text, views, light, facades, night
  scripts/           city-only scripts, named m<N><x>_<what>.py after the milestone that wrote them
  generated/         structure TOML written by those scripts and `include`d by city.toml
  data/              hand-made inputs and notes: landmark CSVs (name, height, coordinates, sources),
                     landmark facade CSVs, research notes in Markdown
```

Left out on purpose:

- **Downloads and derived files.** Boundaries clipped from OSM (`data/boundary_districts.geojson`), the mass
  outlines cut from them (`data/masses_within.geojson`, `masses_ring*.geojson`), Hong Kong's pits and splits:
  the city's `scripts/m1_*.py` (or the pipeline itself) write them again. Where a `city.toml` names one of these
  files, the build fails until you write your own. The two tiny hand-drawn rectangles (Singapore's
  `masses_tall_m7.geojson`, Tokyo's `masses_tall_within.geojson`) are included.
- **`generated/` files over 200 KB** (named in the table): run the script that writes them.
- **Phase 0 maps.** Hong Kong's `scripts/m1_boundary.py` and Tokyo's `scripts/snap_boundary.py` read the
  hand-drawn boundary chosen in Phase 0 from `<project>/plans/maps/<city>.json`; replace that with your own polygon.
- Each city's `README.md`, `REPORT.md`, `checks/` and `eval/` (run logs and screenshots of the original build).

Notes in `data/*.md` were written during the original build and keep its milestone names (M1 data, M2 buildings,
M4 landmarks and facades, M8 night, ...) and its paths: `<project>/demos/data/<city>/raw/` for downloads and
`<scratch>/` for an agent's scratch folder. The data in the landmark CSVs (heights, coordinates) comes from OSM
(ODbL), Wikipedia and official sources cited in each row; check a row's source before relying on it.

## Starting a new city from an example

1. Pick the nearest example **of the same type**, not the nearest place: a tower city on reclaimed land
   (Singapore, Hong Kong), a flat stone capital (Paris, Berlin, London), a dense megacity core (Tokyo, Manhattan).
   The type decides heights, facades, roofs and night lighting more than geography.
2. Make the folders in the default layout, relative to your project root:
   `demos/<city>/` (config, git-tracked) and `demos/data/<city>/` (data, git-ignored).
3. Copy the example's `city.toml`, `pyproject.toml` and `web/city.json` into `demos/<city>/` and
   `demos/<city>/web/`.
4. In `city.toml`, change at least:
   - `name`, `title`, `utm_epsg` (the UTM zone of the city), `drive` (left or right), `coast`, `level_h`,
     `user_agent` (put your own contact in it: Overpass and data hosts ask for one);
   - `[districts]`: the OSM relation ids of your boundary (and any `clip` files you make);
   - `[sources]`: the building and terrain sources of your country (see `../references/data-sources.md`);
     delete the example's own source tables you do not use;
   - `preset`: the nearest region preset, or make your own (`../engine/pipeline/README.md`, "Adding a region preset");
   - `include` and every key naming a file under `data/` or `generated/`: drop them until you have your own;
   - landmark, bridge and structure tables (`[[structures]]`, landmark CSV paths): start empty.
5. In `web/city.json`, change `title`, `nativeTitle`, `about`, `storagePrefix` (a short unique key, or two
   cities served from one host share remembered settings) and the `views` (start with one overview).
6. Link the engine into `web/` and list the data links in `demos/<city>/.gitignore`: see
   `../engine/viewer/README.md`, "Start a new city's web folder".
7. Run `uv sync` and `uv run city3d all` from `demos/<city>/`, then serve `web/` and look.

Keep what you change commented the way the examples are: one line saying why, with the number that decided it.

## `pyproject.toml` paths

Each example's `pyproject.toml` depends on the engine as

```toml
city3d = { path = "../../.agents/skills/build-3d-city/engine/pipeline", editable = true }
```

which assumes the skill sits at `<project>/.agents/skills/build-3d-city/` and the city at `<project>/demos/<city>/`.
If your skill or city lives elsewhere, change that path (relative to the city folder), then `uv sync`.
